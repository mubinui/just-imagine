# Hack2skill x Google "AI Cup" 2026: how the judge panel scores and what a winning entry looks like (as of 2026-10-17)

## Key question 1: Official name, organiser, timeline, phases, tracks, Bangladesh eligibility

### Takeaway

No programme literally called "Google AI Cup" exists on Hack2skill. The programme that matches every signal in the brief (Hack2skill, Google, "Cup", 2026, Asia-Pacific) is the **Google Cloud AI Builder Cup 2026** ("AI Builder Cup JAPAC"), run by Hack2skill and sponsored by Google Cloud, with a prototype deadline of **18 October 2026** (tomorrow relative to this note), registration already closed on 11 October, and an in-person Grand Finale in Singapore on 4 December 2026. I found no Hack2skill x Google programme with a 24 November 2026 deadline; the team's belief about the date appears to be wrong, or refers to something I could not find. Where I needed a comparator I used the Hack2skill x Google Cloud **Gen AI Exchange Hackathon 2025** (India) and the Hack2skill x Google **Solution Challenge 2026** (India), both of which use the same rubric family.

## Key question 2: Official judging criteria and weights

### Takeaway

The AI Builder Cup publishes an explicit weighted rubric, identical across all six themes: **Technical Merit & Gen AI Implementation 40%, Problem Alignment & Impact 25%, Innovation & Creativity 25%, User Experience & Solution Design 10%**, with four separately judged category prizes. The same 40/25/25/10 structure appears in Hack2skill's Solution Challenge 2026, so it is the house rubric for Hack2skill x Google programmes. Google's own Gemini API Developer Competition (2024) scored 65 points across Impact, Remarkability, Creativity, Usefulness and Execution.

### Cited Findings

- T&C wording: "Weighted Scoring Structure: All themes and problem statements will be evaluated using the following explicit weighted rubric: a) Technical Merit & GenAI Implementation (40%): Robustness, Google Cloud architectural depth, and technical execution. b) Problem Alignment & Impact (25%): Clarity of problem-solution fit and measurable real-world viability. c) Innovation & Creativity (25%): Originality and inventiveness of the approach. d) User Experience & Solution Design (10%): Quality, intuitiveness, and clean design of the user interface." — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)
- Themes-page sub-criteria (no sub-weights): under Technical Merit (40%): "Meaningful use of Gen AI" (how effectively generative AI contributes to solving the problem), "Technical implementation" (functional, technically sound, appropriately implemented), "Scalability & sustainability" (potential for wider adoption and future development). Under Problem Alignment & Impact (25%): "Problem alignment" (how well it addresses the chosen theme), "Potential impact" (clear, meaningful value for intended users and stakeholders). Under Innovation & Creativity (25%): "Originality & differentiation" (fresh perspective or meaningful differentiation from existing solutions), "Innovation in the use of Gen AI" (creative application "beyond a basic or superficial implementation"). Under UX & Solution Design (10%): "Usability & accessibility" (intuitive, user-friendly, reasonably accessible), "Experience & integration" (components including Gen AI "work together cohesively"). — [aibuildercup.com/themes.html](https://aibuildercup.com/themes.html)
- FAQ: "The same four weighted criteria apply across all six themes"; there is "a scoring round before the finale" that "produces the shortlist"; "the judging panel to be announced closer to the Grand Finale." — [aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html](https://aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html)
- Category prizes judged separately from the overall ranking: "Best use of Google Cloud AI tools", "Most Impactful Solution", "Social Choice Award", "Best UI/UX", USD 2,000 each; Winner USD 10,000, first runner-up USD 7,000, second runner-up USD 5,000 (total USD 30,000). — [aibuildercup.com/rewards.html](https://aibuildercup.com/rewards.html)
- Judges' decisions are final; the organiser "reserves the right to suspend, amend, or modify any of these rules, terms, and conditions, judging criteria with or without prior notice". — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)
- Solution Challenge 2026 (Hack2skill x Google India) prototype-stage criteria: "Technical Merit: 40%; User Experience: 10%; Alignment With Cause: 25%; Innovation and Creativity: 25%". Phase 2 re-evaluates "scalability, user impact, execution clarity, and solution maturity & Production readiness"; finals: "Judging will be based on innovation, impact, technical depth, scalability, and presentation quality." — [promptwars.in mirror](https://promptwars.in/solutionchallenge2026.html)
- Google's Gemini API Developer Competition (May–Aug 2024) Official Rules, as quoted on Google's developer forum: "Your Submission, including Your video and code will be evaluated based on following judging criteria"; judges score each sub-criterion 1–5, maximum 65 points: Impact (3 criteria, up to 15), Remarkability (2, up to 10), Creativity (3, up to 15), Usefulness (3, up to 15), Execution (2, up to 10, including "Is the solution well-designed and adhere to software engineering practices? (maximum 5 points)"); ties broken by rejudging "for overall video impact". — [discuss.ai.google.dev thread 33566](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/do-we-need-to-submit-the-full-code-how-will-judges-verify-partial-code/33566)
- A Google staff reply on the same forum said winners are judged on "the app judging criteria and the prominent use of the Gemini API". — [discuss.ai.google.dev thread 2275 (search snippet; page now 404)](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/number-of-entries-judging-process-on-usefulness-category/2275)
- Google's older Solution Challenge FAQ scored each solution 1–50, "Impact and Technology are each worth 25 points". — [developers.google.com Solution Challenge FAQ (search snippet; page since replaced)](https://developers.google.com/community/gdsc-solution-challenge/faq)
- Capgemini x Google Cloud agentic hackathon (2025) judged on "innovation, feasibility, and alignment with business needs" with 49 mentors/judges selecting "standout, customer-ready innovations". — [Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/insights/expert-perspectives/agentic-ai-in-action-lessons-from-the-capgemini-and-google-cloud-hackathon/)
- Google Cloud "Agentic Cinema" hackathon (deadline Sept 2026) criteria: technical implementation, product design, potential impact, idea quality. — [Hakky summary](https://book.st-hakky.com/en/news/google-cloud-agentic-cinema-hackathon)

### Inferences

- 65% of the AI Builder Cup score (Technical Merit 40 + Innovation 25) is about _how deeply and inventively generative AI and Google Cloud are used_; only 25% is impact and 10% UX. A beautiful child-facing UI earns at most 10 points plus a shot at the USD 2,000 Best UI/UX prize; "Google Cloud architectural depth" and non-superficial Gen AI use decide the ranking.
- "Measurable real-world viability" (T&C) and "Potential impact … clear, meaningful value" (themes page) mean judges want numbers (sessions, children, minutes of engagement, parent feedback), not a vision statement.
- The Hack2skill house rubric is stable across programmes (40/25/25/10 in both the AI Builder Cup and Solution Challenge 2026), so self-auditing against it is safe even if the team ends up in a different Hack2skill x Google event.

### Gaps

- No scoring guide, anchor descriptions per score band, or per-sub-criterion weights are published; judges' names are not yet announced.
- The official 2025 Gen AI Exchange Hackathon rubric could not be read (JS-rendered page).

## Key question 3: Mandatory submission artefacts and hard requirements

### Takeaway

Hard gates: built "primarily" on Google Cloud and deployed on Cloud Run/GCP/Firebase; fresh code only; one working end-to-end prototype per team under one theme; deliverables are a live deployed URL, a public GitHub repo, a demo video, and a deck covering architecture and business case. The video length is contradictory across official documents (FAQ: under 3 minutes; T&C: strictly 3–4 minutes). No architecture diagram, credits usage report or Vertex-AI-specific requirement is stated, but Gemini/Gemma or a Google agentic platform is expected.

### Cited Findings

- T&C: "Google Cloud Requirement: All submitted prototypes must be built primarily using the Google Cloud tech stack (e.g., Cloud Run, GCP, Firebase). Solutions built primarily on competing cloud infrastructure will be deemed ineligible and disqualified." — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)
- T&C: "Fresh Code Base (No Pre-existing Work): Submissions must consist exclusively of fresh code and original assets created during the official hackathon timeline. Pre-existing products, ongoing projects, or code developed prior to the official launch will be disqualified." — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)
- T&C: "Prototype & Deliverables: Participants must submit a working, end-to-end prototype. Mandatory submission artifacts include: a) A working deployed live URL (on Cloud Run/GCP/Firebase). b) A video demonstration strictly three to four minutes in length. c) A public GitHub repository containing the source code. d) A comprehensive presentation deck explaining the solution architecture and business case." — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)
- FAQ contradicts the video length: required artefacts are "a deployed prototype link, a video demo under 3 minutes, a public GitHub repository, and a deck explaining the solution in detail"; "A working prototype is enough" but it must run end-to-end and be deployed on Cloud Run, GCP or Firebase; "Solutions built primarily on other clouds are not eligible"; "Only projects started during the hackathon timeline qualify." — [aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html](https://aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html). A participant team's 26 Sept transcription also recorded "under 3 minutes" and "Strictly under 3 minutes … Public YouTube, Vimeo or Google Drive link", which suggests the 3–4 minute wording in the T&C is a later edit. — [docs/hackathon/submission-requirements.md](https://github.com/JithinBathula/Google-Hackathon)
- The same transcription of the themes page's submission section lists: "The solution must integrate Gemini-family or Gemma-family models, or be built with a Google agentic platform: Agent Platform, Antigravity or AI Studio"; deployment "on Cloud Run or Firebase"; "English for all materials"; deck (PDF) must cover "the vision and positive impact; how it aligns with the chosen theme; the problem it solves and who benefits; feasibility; and potential to scale"; quality expectations "Functionality… Usability: user-friendly, accessible and scalable… Platform fit: works with the specified Google Cloud tools. - One solution per team under one theme; no multi-theme or multi-team entries. — [aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html](https://aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html)
- The rewards page and FAQ do not mention Google Cloud credits; the FAQ only promises "online sessions covering the program and problem statements". — [aibuildercup.com/rewards.html](https://aibuildercup.com/rewards.html); [aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html](https://aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html)

### Inferences

- For Just Imagine: Cloud Run + Firestore + Vertex AI (Gemini Live, Gemini world-builder) satisfies the Google Cloud and Google-AI gates comfortably. The "fresh code base" clause is the live risk: a project with months of prior commits is, on a literal reading, "code developed prior to the official launch". Be ready to show the repo history started on or after 1 September 2026, or ask the organiser in writing how they treat pre-existing scaffolding.
- Because the T&C says it "shall prevail over any inconsistent terms … in any other promotional or advertising materials", the 3–4 minute video is the legally controlling spec, yet the FAQ and participant guidance say under 3 minutes. A ~3:00–3:15 video is the only length defensible under both readings; confirm on the Discord before export.
- "Google Cloud architectural depth" is scored, so include an architecture diagram in the deck and README even though none is formally required.
- Keep the deployed URL live and stable from 18 October through 6 November (scoring) and, if shortlisted, through 4 December.

### Gaps

- No official statement on API-key handling, authentication for judges (e.g. test accounts), minimum uptime, or whether judges may inspect Cloud Run/Firestore configuration.
- No stated policy on Google Cloud credits for this edition.
- No published guidance on whether non-English (Bangla) UI copy is acceptable; the themes-page transcription says all materials must be in English.

## Key question 4: What previous winners built and what judges praised

### Takeaway

Across Hack2skill x Google finales (Gen AI Exchange 2024 and 2025, Agentic AI Day 2025) and Google's own competitions, winners are **narrow, domain-specific, production-looking tools with agentic or multimodal Gen AI at the core**, deployed and demonstrable live, with a measurable outcome and a human-in-the-loop story. Observers' summary of 2025: a shift toward "domain-specific, production-ready tooling"; winners used agentic systems, explainable AI and RAG.

### Cited Findings

- Gen AI Exchange 2025 winners (one per problem statement): YouthMind (Team Prompt-o-nauts) — daily conversational check-ins producing a "Wellness Risk Index" and personalised activities; ArtisanGully (Oopsie Operators) — agentic platform producing listings, styled images and video ads from photos or voice notes; Legal SahAI (Crewmate) — RAG legal analysis, form assistant, lawyer-client portal; Parallax (Technopaths) — explainable deepfake detection including heartbeat-signal analysis in video; Career & Skills Advisor (Error_404_Not_Found); test-case generation from requirements with Jira integration (Skippers); cement-plant optimisation where "AI agents monitor and optimize plant operations, with human operators approving key decisions" (May The Agents Be With You); PitchLense startup evaluation (AIxpert); multi-language trip planner with booking (Ghostpye); citizen-service governance dual dashboards (Success). — [Hack2skill blog](https://blog.hack2skill.com/gen-ai-exchange-hackathon-finale)
- Judges were "a panel of 30 esteemed industry experts from leading organizations, including Google, NASSCOM, JK Cement, EaseMyTrip, LVX, and others"; keynote by Prashanth Subrahmanyam, APAC Lead, Developer Adoption, Google: "The future of development isn't something you will read about, it's something you will build." ## Key question 5: What disqualifies or sinks an entry

### Takeaway

Formal disqualifiers in the AI Builder Cup T&C are: a student on the team, any member outside JAPAC or under 21, building primarily on a non-Google cloud, pre-existing code, multiple entries, leaving the official comms/dashboard, objectionable or infringing content, malware, and incomplete submissions. Beyond the rules, Google-side guidance and winners consistently flag thin "chat-wrapper" AI use, non-working or non-live demos, missing code, and over-scoping as what sinks entries.

"Entries that do not comply with the provisions of these rules will be deemed ineligible and will not be considered." — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)

Non-compliance results in immediate disqualification without recourse." — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)

- For a children's product, the "objectionable … inconsistent with the sponsors' brand image" clause is the one to fear: a single unsafe AI utterance in a judge's live session can sink the entry regardless of score. Just Imagine's Vertex AI/ADC server-side rule already covers this.
- The "fresh code" and "no advertisements" clauses mean the deck should not read like a SaaS sales page, and the repo should not contain obviously older commit history without explanation.

## Key question 6: Children's edtech: what evidence of impact, safety and pedagogy judges reward

### Takeaway

No Hack2skill or Google rubric contains a child-specific criterion; safety and pedagogy are scored indirectly through "Problem Alignment & Impact", "Usability & accessibility", and the organiser's power to reject "objectionable" content. The best proxies for what a Google judge expects are Google's own five LearnLM pedagogical principles and the Common Sense Media bar Google's own under-13 product was held to: built for children from the start, age-aware responses, consistent filtering, no emotional-support role, protected voice data, parental visibility, and reading level matched to age.

### Cited Findings

- Google's pedagogical framework (Google/LearnLM paper) defines five principles verbatim: "Encourage active learning: Helping learners actively construct understanding through pedagogical approaches like retrieval practice, dialogic inquiry, and problem-solving: tasks in which the learner makes connections, explains concepts, or creates artifacts."; "Manage cognitive load: Directing learner cognitive efforts on the focal learning goal while managing distractions unrelated to the learning goal; using well-structured segments and purposeful dual coding to support working memory and schema construction."; "Stimulate curiosity and motivation: Fostering perceived task value and expectancy of success by anchoring new concepts in prior knowledge, authentic real-world contexts, and learner goals."; "Adapt to the learner: Assessing what the learner knows and can do, providing calibrated challenges and fading scaffolds…"; "Deepen metacognition: Providing explicit opportunities for learners to set goals, plan strategies, monitor comprehension, and evaluate their learning process." Google says "evaluation rubrics derived from these principles served as this quantifiable metric" for "pre-launch evaluations" of its learning products. — [Google pedagogical framework PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/googlespedagogicalframework.pdf)
- Common Sense Media (updated 5 Sep 2025) recommends AI for children be built "for children from the start, rather than adding filters to an adult product"; use "simple interfaces, easy-to-read text, pictures, and interactive features"; give "age-appropriate responses by using the child's known age"; "direct younger children to trusted adults" on sensitive topics; "no user under 18 use chatbots for mental health advice or emotional support"; make filtering "consistent, including across a conversation"; keep "young users' data protected, including voice input"; it credits Google for "not using children's conversations to improve its AI" and for Gemini "saying it's a computer, not a friend". — [Common Sense Media](https://institute.commonsensemedia.org/risk-assessments/gemini-under-13)
- Google's own under-13 rollout (May 2025, via Family Link supervised accounts) came with the commitments that Gemini "has specific guardrails for younger users", that parents are notified on first use and can disable it, and that Google "won't use that data to train its AI"; Google said the offering is COPPA-compliant. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/02/google-will-soon-start-letting-kids-under-13-use-its-gemini-chatbot); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/google-gemini-ai-chatbot-kids-under-13)
- EPIC and Fairplay told the FTC that "COPPA is clear that opt out mechanisms are insufficient" and that under-13 data collection generally needs verifiable parental consent. — [EPIC/Fairplay letter](https://epic.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Letter-to-FTC-re-Google-Gemini_EPIC-and-Fairplay_5.21.25.pdf)
- A children's Gemini product has placed at a Google Cloud hackathon: StoryGrow (ages 3–8, voice/text input, illustrated personalised stories, parent well-being dashboard) took 2nd at ODSC x Google Cloud 2025. — [ODSC](https://opendatascience.com/insights-from-the-winners-of-the-2025-odsc-google-cloud-hackathon/)
- Youth well-being is a repeated Hack2skill x Google winning theme: YouthMind won the 2025 Youth Mental Wellness statement; VAYU won in 2024 with a mental-health tool. — [Hack2skill blog](https://blog.hack2skill.com/gen-ai-exchange-hackathon-finale); [PCE](https://pce.ac.in/?p=7100)
- "Empowering educators" was one of six problem statements at Google Cloud Agentic AI Day 2025, and education is named inside the AI Builder Cup "Media, Content & Digital Experiences" theme and "accessibility" inside "Sustainability & Social Impact". — [Hack2skill blog](https://blog.hack2skill.com/we-have-ourselves-a-guinness-world-record-title); [aibuildercup.com/themes.html](https://aibuildercup.com/themes.html)
- The AI Builder Cup rubric's impact wording is "measurable real-world viability" and "clear, meaningful value for its intended users and stakeholders"; UX wording is "intuitive, user-friendly, and reasonably accessible". — [Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub); [aibuildercup.com/themes.html](https://aibuildercup.com/themes.html)
- Google's 2024 Gemini competition awarded "Most impactful app" to VITE VERE (cognitive-disability support), signalling that accessibility and vulnerable-user impact score well with Google judges. — [ai.google.dev/competition](https://ai.google.dev/competition)

### Inferences

- Pedagogy: map Just Imagine's features to the five LearnLM principles explicitly in the deck (active learning = the child builds the world; cognitive load = three characters and a declarative scene rather than free text; curiosity = physics surprises; adaptation = age band 6–10 vs 11–13; metacognition = the characters asking "what do you think will happen?"). A Google judge who knows LearnLM will recognise the vocabulary.
- Safety evidence that maps directly to the Common Sense bar: no raw audio stored, transcripts discarded at session end, first-name/avatar/age-band-only profiles, Gemini 3.8 Flash moderation of every world patch and image, characters that say they are not real friends and never give emotional-support advice, and a parent dashboard with consent and disable controls. Show the moderation path in the architecture diagram; it doubles as "Google Cloud architectural depth".
- Impact evidence judges can count: number of children/sessions in a pilot (even a few Dhaka families), minutes of sustained engagement, parent-rated outcomes, Bangla vs English usage split, and a before/after on one concrete skill (e.g. predicting what falls faster). Bangla support is a genuine differentiator for "Problem Alignment" in a JAPAC programme whose message is "built for a local need, scaling globally".

### Gaps

- No Hack2skill or Google hackathon rubric mentions COPPA, child data, or age-appropriate design explicitly; no judge has publicly commented on a children's entry at these events.
- No primary Google blog post for the under-13 Gemini launch was retrievable; the safeguards above come from press quoting Google spokespeople.
- No published evidence on how Google judges weigh Bangladesh's Digital Security/Data Protection rules; the T&C references only India's IT Act 2008.

### Inferences

- With 100 teams and 30 judges in one day in Bengaluru, Hack2skill finales run short slots (plausibly 5–10 minutes including Q&A) in parallel panels; Singapore will have fewer teams but the same format pressure. Prepare a 3-minute live demo that works on venue Wi-Fi plus a recorded fallback, and a 90-second architecture explanation.
- Because pitches must match the submitted prototype (Hack2skill norm), the 18 October submission is what the finale is judged against; polish before submission, not after.
- Choose the two travelling members now with Singapore visa feasibility in mind; Bangladeshi passport holders need a visa, and the T&C forbids substitutions.
