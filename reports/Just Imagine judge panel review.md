# Clear the entry gates, then ship Just Imagine

Just Imagine's largest risk is eligibility and timing, not code. The only programme that matches "Hack2skill x Google AI Cup" is the **Google Cloud AI Builder Cup 2026**. Its **prototype deadline is 18 October 2026, which is tomorrow**, not the roughly 24 November the team believes. Registration closed on 11 October and rosters locked on 14 October. Solo entries are barred, every member must be a non-student professional aged 21 or over living in JAPAC (Bangladesh is neither named nor excluded), and the entry must be deployed on Google Cloud. Just Imagine is not deployed anywhere. Against the official 40/25/25/10 rubric it scores **about 46/100 today**, about 62 if the 24-hour plan below lands, and about 81 by the 4 December finale. Those numbers are this report's calibration, not official scores.

The engineering craft is close to winner level: about 1,168 tests, strict TypeScript, a generated-code sandbox that passes its red-team suite, and Terraform that validates. The proof that a judge can check is missing:

- the product runs only on localhost;
- it calls the Gemini Developer API with a key instead of Vertex AI;
- the demo commit has never been through CI;
- the one real build an auditor tried failed after about 35 seconds with no fallback;
- by the team's own measurement, about two minutes pass before any world document exists.

Impact and business are far from winner level: no child has used the product, the plan loses money on its first active child, and Google's own terms bar generative AI in services likely to be used by under-18s. The art is near the top of the field, but the shipped frontend shows Times New Roman where the brand font should be, a privacy page that is still draft text, and an account error when the microphone is blocked. Today the team should email support+aibuildercup@hack2skill.com, then deploy, merge to main and record the video.

No Hack2skill page uses the phrase "Google AI Cup". The programme that fits every signal is the AI Builder Cup 2026 ("Build in JAPAC. Build for the world."). Hack2skill organises it, Google Cloud sponsors it, and it "is not directly administered by Google Cloud" ([aibuildercup.com](https://aibuildercup.com/)). The microsite on 17 October lists:

| Prototype building | 7 September to 18 October |
| **Submission deadline** | **18 October** |

The Terms have been edited at least once mid-programme. Third-party listings still show a superseded 3–4 October deadline. The cut-off time and timezone for 18 October are not published ([Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)). Prizes are USD 10,000, 7,000 and 5,000 for the top three, plus four USD 2,000 category awards ([rewards](https://aibuildercup.com/rewards.html)). Neither the competition researcher nor the product researcher found any Google or Hack2skill programme with a 24 November deadline. The product notes fell back on a "late-November" assumption only because they could not identify the event.

The personal eligibility rules are strict and can be enforced at any stage, including after the finale.

- **Age and status:** every participant must be at least 21 and a working professional, entrepreneur or startup founder.
- **Students:** "If any single member of a team is found to be a student, the entire team will be immediately disqualified, even if discovered after shortlisting, selection, or completion of the Grand Finale."
- **Residency:** every participant must be physically based in, and a legal resident of, a JAPAC country.
- **Verification:** the organiser may check government IDs, employment and age at any time ([Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)).

No document lists the JAPAC countries; the homepage names only Singapore, Indonesia, Japan and Korea. A Pakistani team did represent South Asia at the Google APAC Solution Challenge 2025, which suggests Bangladesh is plausible but does not settle it ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/pakistan-solely-represents-south-asia-at-google-apac-solution-challenge-2025/)).

Team size is 2 to 4 and **solo entries are not allowed** ([FAQ](https://aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html)). The repository shows one committer, `mubinui`, while the spec header describes "Dev A" and "Dev B". The team must be able to show a registered roster of two to four eligible humans.

The **fresh-code rule** says: "Pre-existing products, ongoing projects, or code developed prior to the official launch will be disqualified" ([Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)). It is less dangerous than the competition notes feared. The first commit is dated 2026-10-15, inside the 7 September to 18 October build window, and the design spec carries the same date. The residual exposure is assets rather than code. The same clause requires "original assets created during the official hackathon timeline", and `apps/web/public/learn/sounds` ships third-party CC0 animal recordings that the team's own provenance note says "nobody has listened to yet" (`docs/art/animal-sounds-provenance.md`).

The **Google Cloud gate** is the one the project fails outright. Mandatory artefacts are:

1. a working live URL on Cloud Run, GCP or Firebase;
2. a video "strictly three to four minutes in length";
3. a public GitHub repository;
4. a deck covering architecture and business case.

Solutions "built primarily on competing cloud infrastructure will be deemed ineligible" ([Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)). The FAQ instead asks for "a video demo under 3 minutes" ([FAQ](https://aibuildercup.com/Faqs.html)). The two specifications overlap only at exactly three minutes, so the team should aim for about 3:00 to 3:10. The T&C says it prevails over other materials, so it wins if the organiser does not reply. Submission materials must be in English, and the solution must use Gemini or Gemma ([participant transcription](https://github.com/JithinBathula/Google-Hackathon)).

There is no education theme. Just Imagine fits "Media, Content & Digital Experiences", whose scope names education, or "Sustainability & Social Impact" ([themes](https://aibuildercup.com/themes.html)).

On the project's side, the deployment runbook opens with "No cloud resources have been created by this implementation session" (`docs/runbooks/deployment.md:3`), and `main` does not contain the demo code (see the CI row below).

One more gate is not a competition rule but can act like one. Google Cloud's Service Specific Terms §20(d) say a customer will not use a Generative AI Service in a service "that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" ([Google Cloud service terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms)). The Gemini API terms add an 18-plus developer rule and the same under-18 clause ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). The organiser may disqualify content "inconsistent with the Hackathon or its sponsors' brand image" ([Official T&C](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vRm7ChZ6Ij9fG7uFDkxzUMpwgVeBmnQ6cMDnAIEEX84AiLBOOQ9cYbl3S5OzFBbcVb8TF55s-eVpiXb/pub)). A product for six-year-olds that never mentions §20(d) invites the one question that ends a pitch.

Will each part meet winner level? Previous Hack2skill x Google winners were "domain-specific, production-ready tooling" with agentic or multimodal Gen AI at the core and a live demo ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/google-cloud-gen-ai-hackathon-2025-winners-use-cases-and-what-270000-developers-built)). One prior winner's advice was "One polished feature > five half-finished ones" ([ODSC](https://opendatascience.com/insights-from-the-winners-of-the-2025-odsc-google-cloud-hackathon/)). Measured against that, the four parts stand as follows.

**Engineering is at or near winner level on craft and below it on proof.** The craft is real: zero `@ts-ignore`, emulator-backed Firestore tests, a sandbox that blocked nine of twelve fresh adversarial scripts and let none escape, and least-privilege Terraform that passes `terraform validate`. A judge checking the robustness and "architectural depth" claims finds the opposite: a localhost-only product, a `GEMINI_API_KEY` in `.env.local`, a failed live build, and red CI. Deployment plus an early world publish would close most of the gap.

**Impact is not near winner level.** The impact score sits at the floor. The cause is not dishonesty; the team's internal docs are unusually candid. The cause is that no child has used the product and the problem is never stated. Ten to twelve supervised sessions before the finale would move it to "small but real".

**Business is not near winner level.** At list prices a 10-minute, two-world session costs about $0.90–1.20, so one active child costs $18–24 a month at 20 sessions. The Family plan is $9.99 for up to four children. The plan's own quota ceilings allow more than $320 a month of model spend per family ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Stripe does not onboard Bangladesh-registered businesses ([WorldFirst](https://www.worldfirst.com/sasia/blog/business-banking-insights/stripe-guide-south-asia/)), there is no Taka price, and schools are unbilled. The levers exist: build worlds from a library of pre-made art with one live hero sprite, use Flash for playground worlds, and cap worlds at 3–5 a day. Together they bring a session to about $0.25–0.40. None of these levers is in the pitch.

**The frontend has winner-level art and below-winner polish.** The Learn hosts, Katha and the generated Bengali pond backdrop meet the brief. A design judge will still see Times New Roman on the gallery cards within five seconds.

## Conclusion

The audits change the question from "is Just Imagine good enough?" to "is it eligible and visible?". The engineering moat is real but hidden: a Zod-validated Scene DSL, an AST-checked sandbox whose acceptance gate demands causal evidence that each control works, and a gateway whose admission order cannot be gamed. A judge's first five minutes would surface the opposite story instead: a deadline the team may have misread, a localhost URL, a Developer API key, and a privacy page that admits it is a draft. Almost every P0 is a gap between claim and reality, and a gap of that kind can be closed tonight by deploying the product or rewriting the claim. The deeper latency and continuity work cannot be done before the deadline.

The strategic implication is that this team should compete on Google Cloud depth rather than on breadth. "Best use of Google Cloud AI tools" is its most winnable prize. A deployed, Vertex-backed, multi-model pipeline with a checked-in eval report and a visible trace is rarer among hackathon entries than polished UI, and it carries 40% of the main score. The Bangla-first children's framing is the second asset. It is worth something only if the team answers §20(d) before a judge asks and replaces "no child has used this" with a dozen consented sessions before Singapore.
